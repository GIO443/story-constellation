Luna and the Missing Stone

Luna the rabbit had a way of getting ready for sleep. First she smoothed the moss on her bed. Then she lined up her three favorite things on the windowsill: a feather, an acorn cap, and a small gray stone worn smooth by the river. The stone always went last. That was the rule.

Tonight the feather was there. The acorn cap was there. The stone was not.

Luna looked under the sill. She looked in her slipper, where things sometimes hid. She lifted the moss blanket and patted it flat again. No stone. Her ears drooped, and her heart went tap, tap, tap, faster than a bedtime heart should go.

She sat down in the middle of her burrow and thought. She could search every corner in the dark, and be cross, and be tired. Or she could do what her mother did with worries. Her mother set them on the shelf and told them, "You may wait until morning."

Luna took a slow breath. "You may wait until morning," she whispered to the missing stone, wherever it was.

She smoothed the moss one more time. She lay down. Through the round window she could see the river far away, shining under the moon, the same river that had made her stone so smooth. Maybe the stone missed it. Maybe stones need visits home.

Her ears settled. Her heart went tap... tap... slow and soft.

In the morning she would look behind the water jug, where she had set the stone while washing her paws. But that was a morning thing. The burrow was warm, the moon was kind, and Luna was asleep.
