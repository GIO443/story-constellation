Ben and the Night Fair

All week Ben had begged to see the traveling fair, and all week his parents had said, "Not this year." Then, one night after bedtime, he heard it: music drifting over the rooftops. The fair only opened at night.

His parents were asleep. He eased his window open, climbed down the drainpipe like a squirrel, and set off across town alone. The streets were empty and silver, and being out in them was the finest feeling in the world.

But the front gate was locked. Ben tugged until his arms ached. He tried climbing it and got exactly nowhere, except for a scraped palm. He sat down on the curb, ready to walk home with nothing.

Then he remembered something he had seen that afternoon: the popcorn cart hadn't come in through the front. It had rattled down the little lane behind the hedge. Carts need a cart-sized door. Ben followed the lane in the dark, found the wide back gate propped open for the wagons, and walked straight in.

A man in a long coat at the ring toss liked the look of him. "All on your own, lad? Here — throws are free for brave ones." Ben played until he won a paper dragon, and rode the swing carousel three times under the spinning gold and green lights.

Climbing the fence on the way home he cut his knee, but he was clever about it — long trousers tomorrow, and Mum would never know, and never worry, and never say no next time.

He slipped back through his window just before the birds woke up. The paper dragon went under his pillow.

At breakfast, Mum said, "You look tired, love."

"I had big dreams," said Ben, which was almost true.

Some adventures, Ben decided, are best kept secret — and the bravest explorers always know which ones.
