Sam and the Red Kite

Sam loved his red kite. Every Saturday he flew it in the field behind his house, and every Saturday it came home with him, rolled up neat under his arm.

One Saturday the wind gave a hard tug, the string slipped, and the kite sailed away over the trees.

"I'll find it," said Sam, and he walked into the woods.

The very first tree he checked had the kite in it, resting on a low branch just above his head. It was a tiny bit too high, so he stood on a log, and then it wasn't too high anymore. The kite wasn't torn. It wasn't even dirty.

Tangled in the string was something else: a blue scarf with white stars — the one Mrs. Pine from next door had been missing since the windy day last week.

Sam wanted to fly his kite right away. The field was sunny and the wind was perfect. Instead he rolled up the scarf, walked to Mrs. Pine's gate first, and handed it over the fence. She held it to her cheek and smiled the biggest smile Sam had ever seen on her.

Then Sam went to the field and flew his kite until dinner, and it did not fly away again, and everything was fine.

The end.
