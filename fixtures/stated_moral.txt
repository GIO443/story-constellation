Mia and the Blue Ribbon

Mia and Priya sat together every day at the art table, and everyone knew you could not have one without the other.

When the school art show came, Mia drew a fox in a paper boat. She worked on it for three whole afternoons. But the blue ribbon went to Priya's painting of a rainbow over her grandmother's house.

"You only won because judges always pick rainbows," Mia said, and the words came out sharper than she meant.

Priya's face went still. At lunch she sat with the soccer kids, and the art table felt very big and very empty.

The next day Mia tried to fix it the easy way. She saved Priya a seat and said, "Want to draw?" as if nothing had happened. Priya looked at the seat, said, "No thank you," and walked past.

That night Mia looked at her fox for a long time. Then she turned the drawing over and wrote on the back: "For Priya. Your rainbow was the best one. I'm sorry I said it wasn't."

In the morning she put the drawing on Priya's desk in front of everyone, which was the hardest part. Priya read the back twice. Then she picked up her chair and carried it back to the art table.

And so Mia learned that a real friend is glad for you when you win, and that saying sorry properly costs something — but it is always worth the price.
